## Overview

HapticPipette lets people capture how a real object deforms under pressure and reuse that response when designing mixed-reality experiences. Inspired by pipetting and copy-and-paste interaction, it brings haptic capture into the same MR environment as the design task.

## How it works

1. A finger-worn capacitive force sensor measures applied force, while a head-worn RGB-D camera tracks surface displacement.
2. Egocentric measurement and visual-inertial odometry support capture while the user moves, preserving non-linear force–displacement behavior and hysteresis.
3. The captured curve becomes an interactive element in MR that can be assigned to an object to specify its haptic behavior. Rendering that behavior is outside this paper’s contribution.

## Evaluation & findings

Two user studies assessed measurement performance and interaction usability. The system captured the overall non-linear compliance profiles of 11 materials, including elastic hysteresis, and users found the interaction usable. Three applications explore virtual-object design, augmentation of physical objects, and remote sharing of haptic properties.

## Takeaway

Capturing haptic properties through familiar finger movements can connect everyday materials directly to MR design workflows.
